# Colorado 3 November 2026 income-tax ballot measures, results as of 11 November 2026, and two verification points

Note on status: every vote figure below is unofficial and reported by news media from Colorado Secretary of State (SOS) counts between 3 and 6 November 2026. I found no source stating that any result has been certified, and I could not open the SOS results site or the Blue Book directly. The Ballotpedia measure pages returned empty content to the fetch tool; the Ballotpedia News article did load.

## 1. Which income-tax measures were on the ballot, under what names, and what did each do?

### Takeaway
The two sets of earlier notes describe the same measures and do not conflict: Initiative 195 was numbered Amendment 87 (six-tier graduated income tax, 3.7% to 8.4%) and Initiative 232 was numbered Proposition 136 (statutory cap of income tax rates at 4.4%). Amendment 87 repeals TABOR's uniform-rate requirement and sets the brackets in statute for tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2027.

### Cited Findings
- Initiative 195 would establish a six-tier income tax structure from 3.7% to 8.4% and remove the uniform tax rate requirement from TABOR; it is a combined initiated constitutional amendment and state statute (amends both the constitution and the Colorado Revised Statutes). Supported by Protect Colorado's Future; backers include the Bell Policy Center, Colorado Fiscal Institute, New Era Colorado Action Fund. — [Ballotpedia News, 3 Sep 2026](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/09/03/colorado-voters-will-decide-on-a-graduated-income-tax-initiative-that-competes-with-an-income-tax-cap-measure-in-november/)
- Initiative 232 would cap individual and corporate income tax rates at 4.4% of a taxpayer's federal taxable income; it is an initiated state statute (not constitutional), sponsored by Advance Colorado; the legislature can amend or repeal it. — [Ballotpedia News, 3 Sep 2026](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/09/03/colorado-voters-will-decide-on-a-graduated-income-tax-initiative-that-competes-with-an-income-tax-cap-measure-in-november/)
- "Proposition 136 (formerly Initiative 232, 'Income Tax Cap')" is named as the competing measure to Initiative 195, which at that date was still awaiting its amendment number. — [CPA Practice Advisor, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/09/02/big-change-for-colorado-income-tax-makes-ballot/189508/)
- Post-election coverage uses "Amendment 87" for the graduated income tax and "Proposition 136" for the 4.4% measure, described as a response to Amendment 87. — [Denver7 list of the 14 measures](https://www.denver7.com/news/national-politics/america-votes/how-coloradans-voted-on-the-states-14-ballot-measures-during-the-2026-midterm-election)
- Amendment 87 brackets as reported: 3.7% up to $25,000; 4.2% from $25,000 to $100,000; 4.4% from $100,000 to $500,000; 7.4% from $500,000 to $750,000; 7.9% from $750,000 to $1 million; 8.4% above $1 million. Current law is a flat 4.4%. — [Colorado Sun, 6 Nov 2026](https://coloradosun.com/2026/11/06/amendment-87-results-passes-graduated-income-tax/); same brackets in [CPA Practice Advisor, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/09/02/big-change-for-colorado-income-tax-makes-ballot/189508/)
- Applies to tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2027; the same rate changes apply to business profits; it repeals the constitutional requirement that everyone pay the same rate, restoring a graduated income tax for the first time since 1986. — [Colorado Sun, 6 Nov 2026](https://coloradosun.com/2026/11/06/amendment-87-results-passes-graduated-income-tax/)
- Sun worked examples: a $75,000 household pays $275 less a year; a $550,000 family pays $1,175 more; a $10 million earner about $376,000 more. — [Colorado Sun, 6 Nov 2026](https://coloradosun.com/2026/11/06/amendment-87-results-passes-graduated-income-tax/)
- Revenue: about $2 billion a year, for health care, public schools and child care as the legislature decides. — [Colorado Sun, 6 Nov 2026](https://coloradosun.com/2026/11/06/amendment-87-results-passes-graduated-income-tax/); the Denver Gazette/Colorado Politics article gives $2.7 billion instead — [Colorado Politics, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/06/colorado-passes-graduated-income-tax-eliminates-flat-rate/)
- The measure "amends the state Constitution but touches only a small provision of TABOR"; the constitutional change only removes language. — [Colorado Politics, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/06/colorado-passes-graduated-income-tax-eliminates-flat-rate/); [CPA Practice Advisor, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/09/02/big-change-for-colorado-income-tax-makes-ballot/189508/)
- Vote threshold for Amendment 87: the state title board ruled a simple majority sufficed rather than the 55% normally required for constitutional amendments. — [Colorado Politics, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/06/colorado-passes-graduated-income-tax-eliminates-flat-rate/); also "more than 50%" in [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/09/02/big-change-for-colorado-income-tax-makes-ballot/189508/) and [Aspen Times, 6 Nov 2026](https://aspentimes.com/news/colorado-ballot-measures-midterm-election/); contradicted by [Ballotpedia News, 3 Sep 2026](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/09/03/colorado-voters-will-decide-on-a-graduated-income-tax-initiative-that-competes-with-an-income-tax-cap-measure-in-november/), which said 55% was required.
- Ballot qualification: 130,938 valid signatures against 124,238 required, certified to the ballot by the SOS on 1 Sep 2026. — [Ballotpedia News, 3 Sep 2026](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/09/03/colorado-voters-will-decide-on-a-graduated-income-tax-initiative-that-competes-with-an-income-tax-cap-measure-in-november/); Colorado Politics gives "just over 130,000" against 124,632 required — [Colorado Politics, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/06/colorado-passes-graduated-income-tax-eliminates-flat-rate/)

### Inferences
- "Amendment 87" in one set of notes and "Initiative 195" in the other are the same measure; "Initiative 232" is Proposition 136. Initiative numbers are the pre-qualification Title Board numbers; Amendment/Proposition numbers are the ballot designations.
- The simple-majority threshold is the better-supported reading (three sources including a post-election one citing a title board ruling, and consistent with Colorado's rule that amendments which only repeal constitutional text need a simple majority). Ballotpedia's 55% statement appears to be an error or was superseded, but I could not confirm against the Blue Book.
- The mapping of the six brackets to filing statuses (whether thresholds differ for joint filers) and whether thresholds are inflation-indexed were not stated in any source read.

### Gaps
- Exact constitutional citation (presumably Art. X, sec. 20(8)(a)) and the statutory sections amended (presumably C.R.S. 39-22-104 and 39-22-301): not confirmed from a primary source; the SOS, Blue Book and Ballotpedia measure pages could not be read.
- Whether taxable-income thresholds vary by filing status, and whether they are indexed: not found.
- The $2 billion versus $2.7 billion revenue figures are unreconciled (possibly gross increase on high earners versus net, or different fiscal years).

## 2. Reported results, calls, certification, recount, litigation, and conflict rule

### Takeaway
Amendment 87 is reported passed with about 51.4% (unofficial), called by the Associated Press on 6 November 2026; Proposition 136 is reported defeated with about 48%. Nothing is certified as far as any source shows, and because Proposition 136 failed, the conflicting-measures rule does not come into play.

### Cited Findings
- Election night, 3 Nov: Amendment 87 ahead 50.49% to 49.51% with nearly 1.9 million ballots tallied; described as too close to call. — [Colorado Politics/Denver Gazette, 3 Nov 2026](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/03/colorado-graduated-income-tax-election-results/)
- 5 Nov: backers declared victory while ballots were still being counted. — [Colorado Politics, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/05/amendment-87-backers-declare-victory-as-ballots-continue-to-be-counted/)
- 5 Nov evening (SOS unofficial figures): Amendment 87 passing with 51.37%, margin about 70,836 votes; Proposition 136 failing with about 48% yes, losing by about 138,000. — [Aspen Times, 6 Nov 2026](https://aspentimes.com/news/colorado-ballot-measures-midterm-election/)
- 6 Nov, 10:49 a.m.: Amendment 87 led 51.4% to 48.6%; the Associated Press called it. Voters defeated Proposition 136. — [Colorado Sun, 6 Nov 2026](https://coloradosun.com/2026/11/06/amendment-87-results-passes-graduated-income-tax/)
- 6 Nov morning: 51% yes, lead of roughly 71,000. — [Colorado Politics, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/06/colorado-passes-graduated-income-tax-eliminates-flat-rate/)
- Proposition 136: "over 52%" voting no as of 6 Nov, 10:38 a.m. — [Colorado Politics, updated 6 Nov 2026](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/05/colorado-ballot-measures-what-is-passing-failing-and-undecided/)
- Search-result summaries (not opened pages) cite 51.64% for Amendment 87 and 47.19% for Proposition 136, and 51.4% with about 2.6 million ballots tallied; treat as approximate. — [search listing including Axios Denver, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.axios.com/local/denver/2026/11/06/colorado-ballot-measure-results) (page itself returned HTTP 403)
- Conflict rule: if both had passed, the conflicting provisions of the measure with the most affirmative votes prevail ("the one that receives the greatest number of affirmative votes prevails", quoted from the Colorado Revised Statutes); Ballotpedia noted the constitutional repeal of the uniform-rate requirement could still have taken effect alongside the cap. — [Ballotpedia News, 3 Sep 2026](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/09/03/colorado-voters-will-decide-on-a-graduated-income-tax-initiative-that-competes-with-an-income-tax-cap-measure-in-november/)
- Pre-election litigation: a suit challenging Amendment 87's petition signatures (9,849 in question) was dismissed by Denver District Court, and the Colorado Supreme Court rejected the appeal on Friday 30 Oct 2026, so votes were counted. — [Denver7](https://www.denver7.com/news/politics/colorado-supreme-court-rejects-appeal-to-stop-amendment-87-from-appearing-on-the-november-ballot); [The Sum & Substance](https://tsscolorado.com/judge-denies-effort-to-remove-amendment-87-tax-measure-from-ballot/)
- Governor-elect Phil Weiser opposed Amendment 87 days before the election, calling it "the wrong approach". — [search listing, Colorado Politics coverage](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/06/colorado-passes-graduated-income-tax-eliminates-flat-rate/) (statement appeared in the search summary, not verified in the page text)

### Inferences
- The earlier note "Amendment 87 reported passed with about 51% on 6 November 2026, not yet certified" is accurate. The earlier note listing Initiatives 195 and 232 is also accurate but uses pre-ballot numbers.
- A 2.7-point margin (about 71,000 votes) is far outside Colorado's automatic recount trigger (0.5% of the leading side's votes), so an automatic recount is very unlikely; this is my reasoning from general Colorado law, not a sourced statement.
- Final certified shares may differ slightly from 51.4%; reported figures range from 51.37% to 51.64%.

### Gaps
- No source found stating a certification date or that certification has occurred; Colorado's statutory canvass normally finishes weeks after the election. Not verified for 2026.
- No report found of any post-election lawsuit or recount request concerning Amendment 87 as of 11 Nov 2026; absence of search results is not proof there is none.
- The SOS results page and the CPR and Axios articles could not be opened (403), so exact raw vote totals are missing.

## 3. If a graduated income tax passed: brackets, first tax year, remaining implementation

### Takeaway
Pending certification, Colorado's individual (and corporate) income tax moves from a flat 4.4% to six marginal brackets for tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2027. I found no reporting on Department of Revenue rulemaking or implementing bills; the only next step reported is legislative budgeting of the new revenue.

### Cited Findings
- Bracket schedule (taxable income): 3.7% to $25,000; 4.2% to $100,000; 4.4% to $500,000; 7.4% to $750,000; 7.9% to $1 million; 8.4% above $1 million. First applies to tax years beginning on or after 1 Jan 2027. — [Colorado Sun, 6 Nov 2026](https://coloradosun.com/2026/11/06/amendment-87-results-passes-graduated-income-tax/)
- The same changes apply to business profits (corporate income tax). — [Colorado Sun, 6 Nov 2026](https://coloradosun.com/2026/11/06/amendment-87-results-passes-graduated-income-tax/)
- The measure is self-executing in form: it amends statute directly as well as the constitution. — [Ballotpedia News, 3 Sep 2026](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/09/03/colorado-voters-will-decide-on-a-graduated-income-tax-initiative-that-competes-with-an-income-tax-cap-measure-in-november/)
- Revenue is to go to health care, public schools and child care as the legislature decides; the Joint Budget Committee was due to return the week of 9 Nov to work on a spending plan. — [Colorado Sun, 6 Nov 2026](https://coloradosun.com/2026/11/06/amendment-87-results-passes-graduated-income-tax/)
- Amendment 87 together with Proposition NN is described as letting the legislature spend beyond TABOR's limits. — [Colorado Sun, 6 Nov 2026](https://coloradosun.com/2026/11/06/amendment-87-results-passes-graduated-income-tax/)

### Inferences
- Tax year 2026 returns (filed in 2027) remain on the flat rate; the first graduated returns are for tax year 2027, filed in early 2028. Withholding tables and estimated-payment guidance for 2027 would need updating by the Department of Revenue before January 2027, but no source confirms what has been issued.
- For rules-as-code purposes the rate schedule changes from a single parameter to a bracket table, and existing mechanisms that assume a single rate (for example temporary TABOR rate reductions and any flat-rate withholding formulas) will need to be reconciled with the new schedule. How the statute handles those interactions was not found.
- Because the statutory portion of an initiative can be amended by the legislature, the brackets could be altered by later legislation, though TABOR still requires voter approval for rate increases.

### Gaps
- No Department of Revenue statement, rulemaking notice or withholding guidance found.
- No source on how the TABOR-surplus temporary income tax rate reduction mechanism interacts with the bracket schedule.
- Filing-status treatment and indexing of thresholds not found (see section 1).

## 4. Other tax-related statewide measures on the 3 November 2026 ballot

### Takeaway
Fourteen statewide measures were on the ballot. Besides Amendment 87 and Proposition 136, two were tax-related and both are reported passed: Proposition NN (retain revenue above the TABOR cap for K-12) and Proposition 137 (redirect sporting-goods sales tax revenue to conservation and wildfire work, reducing TABOR refunds).

### Cited Findings
- Proposition NN (legislative referral): lets the state keep and spend up to $4.6 billion a year above the TABOR revenue cap for 10 years for K-12 funding, after which the education spending restrictions lift; passed with about 56%, margin about 266,100 (unofficial, 5 Nov). — [Aspen Times, 6 Nov 2026](https://aspentimes.com/news/colorado-ballot-measures-midterm-election/). Denver7 describes it as directing TABOR refunds to K-12, raising the TABOR cap permanently and funding education programs for 10 years. — [Denver7](https://www.denver7.com/news/national-politics/america-votes/how-coloradans-voted-on-the-states-14-ballot-measures-during-the-2026-midterm-election)
- Proposition 137: redirects existing sporting-goods and outdoor-gear sales tax revenue to conservation and wildfire risk reduction by reducing TABOR refunds in some years; about $175 million a year; passed with about 52%, margin about 83,400 (unofficial, 5 Nov). — [Aspen Times, 6 Nov 2026](https://aspentimes.com/news/colorado-ballot-measures-midterm-election/). Colorado Politics showed it at 50.74% to 49.26% in an earlier count and cites both $137 million and $175 million. — [Colorado Politics, updated 6 Nov 2026](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/05/colorado-ballot-measures-what-is-passing-failing-and-undecided/)
- Non-tax measures, unofficial: Amendments 81 (ICE reporting, ~45%), 82 (natural gas right, ~46%), 83 (hunt and fish, ~45%), 84 (mail-ballot ID, ~36%), 85 (eighth-grade ballot language, ~51% but short of 55%) and 86 (redistricting, ~47%) failed; Propositions 132 (fentanyl, ~53%), 133 (child trafficking, ~62%), 134 (school sports, ~52%) and 135 (surgeries on minors, ~52%) passed. — [Aspen Times, 6 Nov 2026](https://aspentimes.com/news/colorado-ballot-measures-midterm-election/)
- Local tax increases were widely rejected by voters. — [Colorado Politics, 8 Nov 2026](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/08/amid-money-worries-colorado-voters-reject-local-tax-hikes/) (headline only; article not read)

### Inferences
- Proposition NN and Proposition 137 both reduce or eliminate TABOR refunds in coming years, which matters for any code modelling the TABOR refund mechanisms (for example the sales tax refund and temporary rate cut).
- No statewide property tax measure appeared among the 14 listed.

### Gaps
- Exact mechanics of Proposition NN (whether the cap increase is permanent or 10 years) differ between Aspen Times and Denver7; the Blue Book was not read.

## 5. Verification point A: PolicyEngine US and Colorado's Property Tax/Rent/Heat (PTC) rebate

### Takeaway
As of 11 November 2026 the PolicyEngine US repository's Colorado state directory contains no PTC rebate variable or parameter. A full-tree search found only Denver's local property tax relief program, LEAP heating assistance and a property tax exemption subtraction.

### Cited Findings
- `policyengine_us/variables/gov/states/co/` contains: `ccap`, `cdhs` (with `leap`, `tanf`), `co_child_care_subsidies.py`, `doi`, `hcpf` (`chp`, `omnisalud`), `ssa` (`oap`, `state_supplement`, others), `tax` (`income`, `payroll`). There is no `dor` directory and nothing named ptc/rebate at this level. — [GitHub API directory listing, retrieved 11 Nov 2026](https://api.github.com/repos/PolicyEngine/policyengine-us/contents/policyengine_us/variables/gov/states/co)
- `co/tax/income/credits/` contains: `care_worker`, `cdcc`, `co_tabor_cash_back.py`, `ctc`, `eitc`, `family_affordability`, `income_qualified_senior_housing`, `sales_tax_refund`. No PTC rebate. — [GitHub API listing](https://api.github.com/repos/PolicyEngine/policyengine-us/contents/policyengine_us/variables/gov/states/co/tax/income/credits)
- A recursive search of the whole main-branch tree (not truncated) for Colorado paths matching ptc, rent, heat, property_tax or rebate returned only: Denver local `gov/local/co/denver/dhs/property_tax_relief` (variables, parameters, tests), CCAP parent-fee files (false match on "parent"), LEAP heating files, `co_income_tax_rebate_adjustment.py`, and `exemptions/co_property_tax_exemption.py`. — [GitHub API git tree, main, retrieved 11 Nov 2026](https://api.github.com/repos/PolicyEngine/policyengine-us/git/trees/main?recursive=1)

- The state PTC rebate (Form DR 0104PTC) is not modelled; the Denver property tax relief program is a separate city program.
- This was a path-name search; a variable implementing the PTC under an unrelated file name would be missed, but that is unlikely given the repository's naming conventions.

### Gaps
- File contents and open pull requests or issues were not searched, so work in progress on a PTC rebate is not ruled out.

## 6. Verification point B: was the Sales and Use Tax Simplification Task Force extended beyond 1 July 2026?

### Takeaway
Not verified. I found no 2026 legislation extending the task force and no source confirming it was allowed to repeal; the last documented extension is HB20-1022, which set the 1 July 2026 date.

### Cited Findings
- The task force was created by HB17-1216 and extended by HB20-1022 through 1 July 2026. — [Task force final report, Colorado General Assembly](https://leg.colorado.gov/publications/sales-and-use-tax-simplification-task-force-final-report-3); [HB20-1022 bill page](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB20-1022)
- SB25-199 (signed 30 April 2025) suspended the task force's meetings, recommendations and reports for the 2025 interim only; its summary does not change the repeal date. — [SB25-199 bill page](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25-199)
- A statute compilation current "through Fall 2025" still lists C.R.S. 39-26-802 (task force creation) with 39-26-804 "Repeal of part"; it predates the repeal date and so does not settle the question. — [colorado.public.law, C.R.S. 39-26-801](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_39-26-801)
- Two web searches for a 2026 bill (HB26/SB26) continuing the task force returned none; the only 2026 sales-tax bill surfaced was HB26-1223 (software and prepared-food exemptions, signed 4 June 2026), which is unrelated per the search summary. — [Sovos regulatory update on HB26-1223](https://sovos.com/regulatory-updates/sut/co-repeals-downloaded-software-sales-and-use-tax-exemption-effective-january-1-2027/)

### Inferences
- With its 2025 interim suspended and no extension bill found, the most likely state is that part 8 repealed on 1 July 2026, but this is an inference from absence of evidence and should be presented as unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- The General Assembly's 2026 committee page for the task force returned 404 and the bill search could not be queried directly. Confirming requires checking the 2026 Session Laws or the current text of C.R.S. 39-26-804.
