# Twelve span classes decompose Colorado's income tax

Colorado's individual income tax should be decomposed in three layers: **twelve span classes** (the present seven, tightened with closed attributes, plus `limit`, `term`, `index`, `lifecycle` and `nonoperative`), **twenty provision types** that each fix which classes are required, and a **parameter record** that adds unit, keys, edge inclusion and a "how is this number resolved" field to what the repository already stores. The reason for attributes over more classes is empirical: a three-label statute scheme reached token-level agreement of 0.89, while a ten-label legal scheme reached exact-match annotator F1 of about 0.5 at best ([Annotares](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03898); [CuRIAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14719)). The reason new classes are needed at all is that the seven fit only "for years P, rate V applies to base B of persons W, subject to E"; the notes list 23 recurring patterns in the credit, TABOR and apportionment provisions that have no place, and the amended text of the two sections already ingested needs several of them ([HB24-1134](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2024a_1134_signed.pdf); [SB24-228](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2024A/bills/sl/2024a_sl_170.pdf)). No established standard names a rate, a threshold, a tax base or a tax year, so those stay project-defined; the standards supply the names for structure, defined terms, references, time axes and overrides ([USLM User Guide](https://github.com/usgpo/uslm/blob/main/USLM-User-Guide.md); [Akoma Ntoso schema](https://github.com/oasis-open/legaldocml-akomantoso/blob/master/TemporaryRelease20170330Final/akomantoso30.xsd)). The dictionary below is a design synthesised from the notes, not a finding in them, and it is untested: no span has been marked with it, and the statutory wording of 39-22-104(3) and (4), 39-22-105, 39-22-109 and 39-22-110 was never read, so the addition, subtraction, AMT and apportionment types rest on Department of Revenue paraphrase. Throughout, a claim is from a source the researchers read directly unless it is marked "search summary only", which means a lead to be checked, not a source.

## Standards name structure and time, not rates or thresholds

The markup standards settle the structural questions and leave the tax-specific ones open. USLM and Akoma Ntoso both give a lead-in sentence its own element, sibling to the items it introduces (`chapeau`; `intro`), and trailing text its own element (`continuation`; `wrapUp`); in USLM's September 2024 bill samples `<chapeau` occurs 3,058 times ([USLM User Guide §9.4](https://github.com/usgpo/uslm/blob/main/USLM-User-Guide.md); [USLM repository](https://github.com/usgpo/uslm)). Both have `term`, `def`, `ref` and `date`. Akoma Ntoso alone has an inline `quantity` with a `normalized` attribute, declared with no unit, currency or percent attribute, and in its nine bundled examples it appears only in parliamentary debates, never in an act ([Akoma Ntoso schema](https://github.com/oasis-open/legaldocml-akomantoso/blob/master/TemporaryRelease20170330Final/akomantoso30.xsd); [examples](https://github.com/oasis-open/legaldocml-akomantoso/tree/master/csprd02-part2-specs/csprd02-part2-specs-examples)). Neither standard marks "except", "unless" or "notwithstanding"; the only exception-like element is the "Provided that" proviso. **Rate, threshold, base, tax year, credit amount and phase-out have no element in any standard read**, so the repository's names for them are local inventions and should be documented as such.

Three borrowed distinctions do real work. First, time has three axes. Akoma Ntoso separates the periods of `force`, `efficacy` and `application`, and LKIF names `In_Force_Interval`, `Efficacy_Interval` and `Application_Interval` ([Akoma Ntoso schema](https://github.com/oasis-open/legaldocml-akomantoso/blob/master/TemporaryRelease20170330Final/akomantoso30.xsd); [LKIF Core](https://github.com/RinkeHoekstra/lkif-core)). Colorado needs exactly that split: the family affordability credit applies to tax years before 2034 and its section is repealed at the end of 2037; the 2024-only EITC paragraph is repealed at the end of 2034 ([HB24-1311](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2024A/bills/sl/2024a_sl_293.pdf); [39-22-123.5 mirror](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_39-22-123.5)). Tax years covered, repeal date and act effective date are three different facts. Second, overrides are first-class: LegalRuleML records `<lrml:Override over="#r2" under="#r1"/>` and Catala makes each exception name the labelled definition it displaces, aborting if two definitions apply at once ([Lam & Hashmi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06128); [Catala, ICFP 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03198)). The repository's `conflict_note` is an informal override; a structured form names the prevailing source, the displaced source and the ground. Third, the party who bears a rule is distinct from a party merely mentioned: LegalRuleML has `Bearer`, Sleimi et al. separate Agent from Target, and the lease-contract scheme of Sancheti et al. ties each deontic label to an agent and reached Krippendorff's alpha of 0.65 for deontic type ([Sleimi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.11245); [Sancheti et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12752)). Among the deontic labels found, "Entitlement" sits nearest a tax credit; mapping a credit to LegalRuleML `Right` is the note-taker's interpretation, since no source read discusses credits.

The annotation literature fixes the shape of the dictionary. Its consistent pattern is classify the unit, then extract type-specific slots: MAUD's deal-point type decides which questions apply, SARA casts each tax subsection as a predicate with slots, and STARA classifies each small statutory unit and then extracts named fields ([MAUD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00876); [SARA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05257); [STARA](https://reglab.github.io/stara)). The Biagioli and Francesconi model is described the same way, but search summary only. A second pattern is a verbatim span paired with a closed-value answer: CUAD releases a normalised or yes/no answer beside each clause, and LangExtract keeps `extraction_text` verbatim, puts interpretation in `attributes`, and can restrict an attribute to an enum ([CUAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06268); [LangExtract output_schema](https://github.com/google/langextract/blob/main/docs/examples/output_schema.md)). A third is that extraction, not classification, is the weak step: full STARA scored 0.98 precision on classification and 0.76 on extraction, and context moved extraction from 0.32 to 0.70 where prompt wording moved it from 0.28 to 0.32 ([STARA](https://reglab.github.io/stara)). SARA's counts say what a tax dictionary must reach: of its simplified Internal Revenue Code subsections, 62 refer to time, 50 state an exception or substitution, 55 need numerical reasoning, and implicit cross-references to other sections (44) outnumber explicit ones (34) ([SARA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05257)). No public benchmark tests extraction of rates, amounts or dates from statute text, so the repository's hand marks are the only reference.

The rules-as-code systems show what a parameter store omits. PolicyEngine's `metadata` is an open dictionary whose unit vocabulary (`currency-USD`, `/1`, `year`, `bool`, `list`) is convention; several Colorado child tax credit files give an amount scale's unit under `rate_unit`, so it never reaches the values. Bracket edge inclusion lives in the formula (`.calc(agi, right=True)`), the exclusive sense of an age limit lives in description prose, and the instrument that set each value, forecast status and agency departures from statute live in YAML comments ([policyengine-us Colorado parameters](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-us/tree/ca7a4702718ca5d0a3f544cd73535e882d501930/policyengine_us/parameters/gov/states/co/tax/income); [co_ctc.py](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-us/blob/ca7a4702718ca5d0a3f544cd73535e882d501930/policyengine_us/variables/gov/states/co/tax/income/credits/ctc/co_ctc.py)). PolicyEngine stores projected TABOR tiers as ordinary values and carries the last value forward indefinitely; OpenFisca's `expected:` key, which both loaders ignore, is the closer precedent for this repository's rule that an unknown value stays unrecorded ([openfisca-doc](https://github.com/openfisca/openfisca-doc/blob/35577e3c38b3439695df77dde859ea21a7c99125/source/coding-the-legislation/legislation_parameters.md); [policyengine-core parameter.py](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-core/blob/7c84d941be767309e1c24381a67635b8b8bf458d/policyengine_core/parameters/parameter.py)). FLINT fills frame slots with text fragments from the sentence plus a versioned source reference, the nearest precedent for a span dictionary, but that description is search summary only; what was read directly is that FLINT has three frame classes (acts, facts, duties) and exists to compare interpretations ([Lutz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.05893)). Better Rules' sequence of concept model, decision model, rule statements, then code argues for a term list before parameters, again search summary only ([digital.govt.nz](https://digital.govt.nz/blog/what-is-better-rules)).

## Seven classes become twelve, each with closed attributes

The seven current classes are overloaded, not wrong. `value` holds only percentages so far and will need dollars, deltas and replacements; `threshold` is defined as an income amount but the statute also tests ages, aggregate state revenue and a growth rate; `base` is currently used for an ordering rule in 39-22-104(2); and `effect` appears once. The table keeps all seven names, gives each a `kind`-style attribute with closed values, and adds five classes for things no attribute can absorb. Definitions follow CUAD's form, a question plus an inclusion sentence, because CUAD reuses the same text as the model prompt and the one comparison found suggests a plain statement of the guideline outperforms an elaborated prompt ([CUAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06268); [Annotares](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03898)).

| Class | Definition | Closed attributes | Normalised form |
|---|---|---|---|
| `period` | Which span of time does the rule apply to or measure? Includes tax years, state fiscal years, event windows, official deadlines and durations. Excludes repeal and effective dates (`lifecycle`). | `kind`: tax_year, fiscal_year, event_window, deadline, duration | `{from, to}` ISO dates, inclusive, `null` when open; a list for a union; ISO duration for `duration` |
| `value` | What number does the provision set? Includes rates, shares of another amount, dollar amounts, counts, and changes to any of these. | `unit`: rate, usd, count, year; `role`: level, delta, replacement, derived; `per` (optional): child, policy, beneficiary, return; `indexed`: true or false | Decimal string, 1.0 = 100%; signed for a delta; `{from, to}` for a replacement |
| `base` | What quantity is the number applied to, or does a formula operate on? Includes federal amounts by reference and counterfactual versions of them. | `measure`: federal_taxable_income, federal_agi, modified_agi, federal_credit, expense, state_excess_revenue, credit_amount, other; `role`: multiplicand, numerator, denominator, divisor; `counterfactual`: true or false | `refs`: list of cited provisions, including any limitation disregarded |
| `who` | Which person or body does the rule address or count? Includes taxpayers, the unit a credit is counted per, and the official who must act. | `role`: taxpayer, unit, official; `filing_status`: single, joint, any; `residency`: resident, part_year, nonresident, any | None; for `official`, a canonical name |
| `threshold` | At what level of a tested quantity does a result start, stop or change? Includes bracket edges, eligibility gates, phase-out starts and steps, trigger tiers, ages and day counts. | `tests`: federal_taxable_income, federal_agi, modified_agi, age, days, state_excess_revenue, adjustment_factor, unemployment_rate, permanent_rate; `role`: bracket_edge, gate, phase_out_start, step, trigger, materiality; `measured_at` (ages only) | `{lower, lower_inclusive, upper, upper_inclusive}` |
| `limit` | What bound caps or floors a computed amount? Includes "but not below zero", "cannot exceed", "the greater of", "whichever is smaller". | `kind`: cap, floor, lesser_of, greater_of | Decimal string, or `refs` to the competing amount |
| `exception` | Which cross-reference displaces, conditions or stacks on this provision? | `kind`: displaced_by, subject_to, modified_by, in_addition_to, notwithstanding, carve_out | `refs` |
| `effect` | What legal consequence follows, other than a number? | `kind`: imposed, allowed, denied, added, subtracted, refunded, carried_forward, recaptured, apportioned, ordering, benefit_disregard, duty | For carried_forward, years as an integer; for apportioned and ordering, `refs` |
| `term` | Which word or phrase is being defined here? | `mode`: means, includes, excludes, same_as | `refs` when the meaning is imported |
| `index` | Which external series or computed factor drives an adjustment or trigger? Includes a named price index and a defined growth factor. | `kind`: price_index, spending_growth, revenue_growth, labour_statistic | Canonical series name and publisher |
| `lifecycle` | What bounds the existence or force of the text itself? Includes repeal clauses, effective-date clauses and contingencies on a legal event. | `kind`: repeal, effective, contingent | ISO date; for contingent, the event and `refs`; `target` provisions |
| `nonoperative` | Which text sets no taxpayer-facing rule? Includes declarations, tax preference performance statements, appropriations, reporting duties and payment-method authority. | `kind`: declaration, performance_statement, appropriation, reporting, administrative_authority | None; numbers inside are never parameters |

These twelve, together with the provision type (which carries the operation: impose, add, subtract, credit) and the provision's status (which carries new against struck text), account for all 23 uncovered patterns in the notes. Relations ride on a shared `group` attribute, the device LangExtract documents for tying a dosage to its medication ([LangExtract medication examples](https://github.com/google/langextract/blob/main/docs/examples/medication_examples.md)). The family affordability phase-out, "six and eight hundred seventy-five one-thousandths percent for each five thousand dollars by which ... adjusted gross income exceeds fifteen thousand dollars", becomes one `value` (rate, level, 0.06875) and two `threshold` spans (step 5000; phase_out_start 15000) in one group, with "but not below zero" as a `limit` floor ([HB24-1311](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2024A/bills/sl/2024a_sl_293.pdf)). An EITC trigger tier becomes a `threshold` testing adjustment_factor grouped with a `value` whose role is delta. This generalises the repository's `derive: schedule` note, which today pairs rates and thresholds by position in the Amendment 87 bracket list.

### Normalisation rules that deterministic code can enforce

Colorado statutes spell every number in words; digits appear only in appropriation clauses and declarations, which is itself a cue for `nonoperative` ([HB24-1311](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2024A/bills/sl/2024a_sl_293.pdf)). The number normaliser must separate three roles the surface form distinguishes. A level: "four and forty one-hundredths percent" gives 0.044, "six and eight hundred seventy-five one-thousandths percent" gives 0.06875, and the simple-fraction form "four and one-half percent" gives 0.045. A delta: "reduced by four one-hundredths of one percent" gives −0.0004, where the trailing "of one percent" is what makes it 0.04 of a point and not 4 percent, and "increased by fifteen percentage points" gives +0.15. A replacement: "from four and forty one-hundredths percent ... to four and twenty-five one-hundredths percent" gives `{from: 0.044, to: 0.0425}` ([SB24-228](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2024A/bills/sl/2024a_sl_170.pdf); [HB24-1134](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2024a_1134_signed.pdf)). The parser must accept the unhyphenated ballot-measure style ("TWENTY FIVE THOUSAND DOLLARS") ([Amendment 87 text](https://leg.colorado.gov/ballots/graduated-income-tax-2/files/text-of-measure)). The 1.0 = 100% convention follows OpenFisca's `/1` unit ([openfisca-doc](https://github.com/openfisca/openfisca-doc/blob/35577e3c38b3439695df77dde859ea21a7c99125/source/coding-the-legislation/legislation_parameters.md)).

The period normaliser must treat four vocabularies as one ("taxable years" and "income tax years"; "commencing" and "beginning"; "on or after" and "on and after"; "prior to" and "before") and handle seven shapes: closed range, open start, open end, single year, a union in one sentence, a fiscal-year range, and a rule mapping a fiscal year to a tax year ("the income tax year commencing during the calendar year in which the state fiscal year ended"). The repository's parser already covers the first five. "Before January 1, 2023" normalises to an inclusive end of 2022-12-31, as the existing marks do. The fiscal-to-tax-year mapping is a rule, not a date, and belongs in an attribute on the `period` span.

Comparators normalise to inclusive flags, which matters because PolicyEngine leaves edge inclusion out of the parameter. "Less than or equal to", "or less", "not more than" and "up to and including" set an inclusive upper bound; "exceeds", "greater than" and "over" set an exclusive lower bound; "equal to or greater than" and "reaches" set an inclusive lower bound; "under six years of age" is an exclusive upper bound of 6. Older text writes off-by-one edges, "between twenty-five thousand one dollars and thirty-five thousand dollars", which normalise to both bounds inclusive ([HB24-1134](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2024a_1134_signed.pdf)). An age threshold records `measured_at`, since the statute says "at the close of the income tax year" while the TABOR refund uses "as of January 1" ([HB23-1112](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2023a_1112_signed.pdf); [2025 104 Book](https://tax.colorado.gov/sites/tax/files/documents/Book104_2025.pdf)). `tests` must name the income measure, because three gate different provisions: federal taxable income, federal AGI, and two different "modified AGI" figures on DR 0104 and DR 0104PN.

### Boundary rules, and why they are strict

Boundaries are where both people and models fail. In Annotares a model reached 0.772 token accuracy on one class and matched zero spans exactly; in adjudication, 84.4% of cases were identical or fixed by deterministic correction of trivia such as punctuation at span edges ([Annotares](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03898)). Five rules follow. A span is the shortest run of words that still reads on its own, ContractNLI's "as concise as possible ... while being self-contained" ([ContractNLI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01799)). It carries no leading or trailing punctuation, article or connective, but a `value` includes its unit word ("percent", "dollars"), a `threshold` includes its comparator words, and an `exception` includes its opening words ("Except as otherwise provided in"). Spans do not overlap: LangExtract's prompt says "Do not paraphrase or overlap entities", and on repeated passes the first extraction wins, so a reference inside a `base` is recorded in that span's `refs`, not as a nested span ([LangExtract README](https://github.com/google/langextract/blob/main/README.md)). A span never crosses a provision boundary; when the meaning is split between a lead-in and a list item, each part is marked in its own unit and the group cites both, which matches the standards' practice of making the chapeau separately addressable. Repeated text is marked at each occurrence in order, except in a restating list such as the Amendment 87 brackets, where the repository's existing rule of first appearance stands.

Three scope decisions belong in every class definition because leaving them out produced measurable error in the legal RAG benchmark: state the evaluation window, say whether expired but still codified provisions count, and say whether enumerated exceptions control ([Benchmarking Legal RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03300)). For this dictionary: spans are marked in every provision whose `status` is in force or repealed-but-ingested, with the provision's status carried on the group; a period limited to past tax years is still a `period`; and an exception is always its own span, never part of the span it qualifies. An annotator, human or model, also needs an "unsure" flag that excludes the span from scores, as Annotares used.

## Twenty provision types fix which spans are required

A provision is typed first, and the type fixes the required classes; a validator can then enforce presence in code, which matters because models did not reliably honour Annotares' condition-needs-a-consequence rule from the prompt alone ([Annotares](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03898)). The unit is the smallest enumerated provision, with period, who and exception inherited from its lead-in when absent, following STARA's practice of supplying each unit with its chapeau and scoped definitions; that description comes from this repository's earlier note on the STARA paper, not a re-read. Colorado drafting is formulaic enough to make this workable: a credit section runs period, eligible person, an allowance verb ("is allowed ... against the taxes due under this article 22"), a number in words, a base often defined by federal reference, then short subsections for refund, part-year apportionment, benefits interaction and repeal.

Three features of the table deserve defence. The `trigger` and `temporary_rate_adjustment` types exist because the rate now has three layers: the permanent rate in 39-22-104, a reduction computed from certified excess revenue under 39-22-627 in seven tiers from 0.04 to 0.15 of a point, and a bracket schedule if Amendment 87 takes effect ([SB24-228](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2024A/bills/sl/2024a_sl_170.pdf)). PolicyEngine merges the first two into one "rate on the form" series; keeping them as separate types preserves what the statute actually says. The `nonoperative` type is the dictionary's explicit "none" class, the device CUAD, ContractNLI and Annotares each use in some form; without it an extractor files "Eighty-three percent of Colorado parents" or "$178,494 is appropriated" as values. And `addition`, `subtraction`, the AMT (no type is proposed for it) and the statutory form of `apportionment` are provisional: their required classes are inferred from Department forms. The pension subtraction, for instance, is "$24,000 minus the Social Security subtraction", a limit reduced by another line, and whether the statute words it that way is unconfirmed ([2025 104 Book](https://tax.colorado.gov/sites/tax/files/documents/Book104_2025.pdf)). Penalty and interest rules were seen only in agency text and get no type.

For the two sections already ingested, the new types have immediate work. The amended heading of 39-22-123.5 reads "Earned income tax credit - not a refund of excess state revenues - trigger - legislative declaration - repeal", so marking the current section needs `trigger`, `delegation`, `lifecycle_clause` and `nonoperative`; the repository's ingested copy has no trigger text at all. Subsections (5) and (6) and the (2.5) and (2.7) variants, ingested but unmarked, become `apportionment`, `benefit_disregard` and `credit_allowance` with a counterfactual `base` ("would have been allowed, but for the fact that ..."). The existing mark on 39-22-104(2) moves from `base` to `effect` (ordering).

## Parameter records need unit, keys and a resolution kind

The repository's parameter record already carries more than PolicyEngine's: a closed interval per value, a status from a four-value list, sources by kind with a checked quote, and a `conflict_note`. The notes support six additions. A closed `unit` (rate, usd, year, count, bool), enforced by the validator because the open convention demonstrably drifts. A `shape` drawn from the kinds PolicyEngine's Colorado tree actually uses: scalar, marginal_rate schedule, single_amount lookup, phase_out (start, step, rate), switch, list. A `keys` list, since Department tables are keyed by tax year and often by filing status and an income or age band, with a note on collapse: the child tax credit treats head of household and married filing separately as "single" ([Income Tax Topics: Child Tax Credit](https://tax.colorado.gov/income-tax-topics-child-tax-credit)). Explicit `lower_inclusive` and `upper_inclusive` on every band edge, and `per` on every per-unit amount. A `form_line` of form, year and line, never a bare number, because the TABOR refund sits on line 33, 34, 35 and 38 of DR 0104 in 2022 through 2025. And a `resolution` field, the most important.

**Few Colorado numbers stand alone, and the statute often never contains the operative number.** The notes identify five kinds of outside dependence with different timing: a federal figure known at filing; state excess revenue certified after the fiscal year; a December forecast before the tax year; a price index applied annually by the Department; and a legal event such as an election or a programme's repeal. A sixth is an amount fixed by another agency's certificate. The proposed values for `resolution` are therefore statute_fixed, federal_reference, certified_revenue, forecast_trigger, index_applied, agency_certificate and legal_event, with `resolved_by` naming the publisher of the final number. For an indexed limit, statute and agency are both right: the act sets $75,000 and $85,000, and the Department publishes $77,000 and $87,000 for 2025 after applying the Denver-Aurora-Lakewood index under a rule that adjusts only when a limit moves by at least $1,000 after rounding ([HB23-1112](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2023a_1112_signed.pdf); [Income Tax Topics: Child Tax Credit](https://tax.colorado.gov/income-tax-topics-child-tax-credit)). That is not a conflict and should not need a `conflict_note`; the EITC's 2023 figure, 25 percent in the mirror statute copy against 50 percent in the Department's table, is one, and the act that explains it was not read. Under the repository's rule that unknown values stay unrecorded, every future year of the EITC percentage, the family affordability credit, the TABOR refund and any temporary rate is unrecorded by default. A forecast figure, if kept at all, belongs in an inert `expected` field.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the task. The hard part of Colorado's income tax is not vocabulary for rates and brackets, which the seven classes already handle, but that the statute increasingly delegates its numbers: to a revenue certification, a forecast, a price index, another agency, or the voters. A dictionary that only marks numbers in statute text will ground a shrinking share of what the return actually computes. The `resolution` field, the `trigger`, `indexing_rule` and `delegation` types, and the `index` and `lifecycle` classes are what let the repository say, for each year, whether a number is in the statute, derivable from it, or published only by the Department, and refuse to compute in the last case until a stored copy says so.

Three checks should come before adoption. Read the official text of 39-22-104(3) and (4), -105, -109 and -110 and confirm or revise the provisional types. Mark the amended 39-22-123.5 and 39-22-627 with the twelve classes and count how often each class and attribute value is used, since the notes have no frequency data and a class that never fires should be cut. And score the marks three ways, exact span, overlapping span and normalised value, because the engine consumes the value and a boundary off by a comma is not a wrong parameter. Whether twelve classes with attributes reproduce better than seven flat ones is an inference from other projects' agreement figures; no study isolates it, and none measures run-to-run stability of span boundaries for one model and prompt.
